Principles

Seven rules that cover the cases nobody wrote down

Calm is the feature

Fewer things on screen, more space, no urgency tricks. After every action, say what happens next.

Answer first

Lead with the one fact the person came for, in big type. Details come after.

One basil action per screen

Basil marks the single next step. Two basil buttons means one is wrong.

A color means one thing

Mint is booked, apricot is filling, sky is information, red is a problem. Always.

Surfaces, not outlines

Separate things with space and a change of surface. Borders only where an edge must be seen.

The table is the product

Show seats, not statistics. The seat row goes anywhere capacity matters.

Readable at every age

16px body, 44px targets, AA contrast everywhere, no pale grey text.

Functional to expressive

One brand, dialed up or down

Every piece sits on one scale. The foundation never changes; expression is added only where the piece can afford it. When unsure, go one step more functional. Owners trust calm.

        Do

        • Lead every owner screen with the answer in big type.
        • Put the seat row on every event and space card.
        • Use one basil button per screen.
        • Follow every action with what happens next.
        • Separate things with space and surfaces.
        • Keep each status color to its one meaning.

        Don't

        • Put borders or shadows around cards, chips or panels.
        • Use soft colors as text, icons or thin lines.
        • Use bright yellow, orange or anything that reads as an alarm.
        • Use red for anything but a problem.
        • Add urgency: countdowns, "hurry", exclamation marks.
        • Animate anything the person didn't cause.
