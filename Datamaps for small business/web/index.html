One command. A map, then a conversation.

Type /datamap and say what to map. Claude does the rest, and then stays with you to work out what the map means for your business.

01 · Ask

Say what to map

A reviews or survey export, a folder of notes, your inbox, or your website.

02 · Gather

Claude reads it

It reads pages and files itself and tidies them into clean notes (Open Knowledge Format), fixing what AI tools can't read.

03 · Map

Similar things land together

On your computer, every note becomes a dot; dots about the same thing form regions.

04 · Talk

Ask what it means

Claude names every region in plain words, writes a short report, and answers your questions by pointing at the map.

The mapping is a "King Tutte pipeline": UMAP, HDBSCAN and DataMapPlot, open-source tools from the Tutte Institute for Mathematics and Computing.

Map your own business

Anything with words in it works. Most tools already have an "Export to CSV" button.

Reviews & surveysGoogle, Yelp, Shopify or Typeform exports. Ratings and dates are used automatically.
Notes & wikisAn Obsidian vault, a folder of Markdown, a "second brain", an OKF bundle.
Your websiteClaude reads the pages itself. Each dot links back to the live page.
DocumentsA folder of PDFs or text files: proposals, reports, meeting notes.

Set up once

  1. Get Claude Code, in the Claude desktop app or the terminal. Any paid Claude plan works.
  2. Ask Claude: "Install the /datamap skill from github.com/Connoiter/claude_for_small_businesses_hackathon". It downloads the project and sets everything up. No ZIP, no code.
  3. Type /datamap in any folder and say what to map.

Mapping runs on your computer. Claude reads short samples from each region to name it.

# Once: ask Claude to install it
Install the /datamap skill from https://github.com/Connoiter/claude_for_small_businesses_hackathon

# Then, in any folder
/datamap reviews.csv
/datamap ~/Notes/my-vault
/datamap https://yourbusiness.com

# Or ask in plain words
What are my customers saying in reviews.csv?
