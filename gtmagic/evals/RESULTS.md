# GTMagic results

All runs use Claude models only, at `reasoning_effort: high`. Every prompt, model and input is recorded in [benchmarks/RUNLOG.md](benchmarks/RUNLOG.md), and every number below is regenerated by code from the files in this folder.

## 1. Beats Claude Opus at predicting how each audience rates different marketing concepts

GTMagic's pipeline (Claude Opus plus a simulated customer panel) predicts how each audience group rates different marketing concepts with **16% lower error than Claude Opus alone**, on held-out real survey data. It is **better on 13 of 18 audience groups.**

| Held-out test: 120 real US adults, 18 audience groups, 18 news-post concepts | Correlation with each group's real average ratings | Mean absolute error (1–7 scale) |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Opus (high), asked for each group's average rating | 0.831 | 0.326 |
| **GTMagic pipeline** | **0.871** | **0.274** |
| Pipeline − Opus, paired 95% bootstrap CI | +0.041 [+0.014, +0.069] | −0.052 [−0.091, −0.008] (**−16%**) |

- **Data:** the `infotainment` study of the [Twin-2K-500 Mega-Study](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LLM-Digital-Twin/Twin-2K-500-Mega-Study) (Apache 2.0). 300 real people rated how likely they'd be to share 18 news posts, which vary by headline, source, content type, political lean and likes. People are split 120 / 60 / 120 into train / val / test, and the test split was used once.
- **Pipeline:**
  1. **Simulated panel:** one Claude Sonnet (high) simulated customer per person, built from census-style demographics only.
  2. **Panel average:** the panel's ratings are averaged within each audience group.
  3. **Blend:** the panel average is blended 50/50 with Claude Opus's prediction. The weight was fixed in advance, not tuned.
- **Audience groups:** a group is scored when it has at least 25 people: party, ideology, age, sex, education, income and race groups, plus "all".
- **Reproduce:** `scripts/pipeline_blend.py`.
- **Validation split:** pipeline − Opus is +0.095 in correlation and −29% error, better on 7 of 7 groups.

## 2. Tops every published result on the Twin-2K-500 Mega-Study benchmark

On the same held-out test, GTMagic outperforms every published model result that ships with the dataset (the dataset authors' digital-twin runs), re-scored with the same metric.

| Held-out test | Correlation with groups' real ratings | Mean absolute error |
|---|---|---|
| **GTMagic pipeline** | **0.87** | **0.27** |
| GTMagic simulated panel alone | 0.86 | 0.34 |
| Best published result | 0.82 | 0.46 (best published error) |
| Other published results (5) | 0.49–0.73 | 0.50–1.34 |

Full table: [benchmarks/megastudy/RESULTS.md](benchmarks/megastudy/RESULTS.md) (published runs are the `ref-*` rows).

## 3. The decision step picks the better real headline 68% of the time

Given "Here are two advertisements I made. Which one would be better?", GTMagic's decision step (Claude Opus, high) picked the headline with the higher real click-through rate in **68 of 100** real A/B tests (95% CI 58–77%), against 50% for chance.

- **Data:** [Upworthy Research Archive](https://osf.io/jd64p/) (CC BY 4.0), via the pairwise holdout split of `Yanjo/headlines-ctr`.
- **Sample:** 100 pairs, one per article, each with the same image and two headlines whose CTRs differed significantly (p < .05, at least 1,000 impressions each). A/B order was randomized.
- **Reproduce:** `benchmarks/upworthy-pairs/pairs.py score`.
